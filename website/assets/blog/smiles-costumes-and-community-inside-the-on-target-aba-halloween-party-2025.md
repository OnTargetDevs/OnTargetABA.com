### Introduction: A Celebration of Fun and Friendship

Every October, the halls of On Target ABA come alive with excitement — and this year’s On Target ABA Halloween party was one to remember! 🎃👻

From Cleveland to Columbus, from Worthington to Utah, our centers were filled with laughter, creativity, and connection. It wasn’t just about costumes and candy — it was about community, confidence, and joyful learning for every child and family we serve.

At On Target ABA, we believe therapy can be both meaningful and fun — and events like this prove it. 💙

### Why We Celebrate Halloween Together

Holidays can sometimes feel overwhelming for children with autism, with new sights, sounds, and routines. But when celebrated thoughtfully, they can also become powerful opportunities for social growth and emotional learning.

The On Target ABA Halloween party was designed with those goals in mind — creating a safe, sensory-friendly space where every child could participate, connect, and shine.

Our team carefully planned each activity to build skills like:

- Turn-taking through games and group play

- Communication during costume sharing and storytelling

- Social interaction through structured group activities

- Confidence in joining a community celebration

Because when kids feel safe and supported, magic happens. 🌟

### Building Social and Emotional Skills Through Play

The On Target ABA Halloween party wasn’t just fun — it was also a valuable learning opportunity.

Through costume sharing, collaborative games, and group activities, children practiced:

That’s exactly what we provide at On Target ABA — across all our locations in:

🧩 Social communication — greeting friends, taking turns, and engaging in group fun
🎭 Flexibility — adjusting to a new routine and environment
🗣️ Expressive language — describing costumes, asking questions, and storytelling
💪 Confidence — celebrating accomplishments and receiving praise

Our BCBAs and RBTs designed the event to balance excitement with structure, ensuring that every activity aligned with core ABA goals while keeping things lighthearted and inclusive.

“Events like this help our clients practice real-life skills in fun, natural ways,” said one therapist. “And seeing their smiles — that’s the best progress of all.”
