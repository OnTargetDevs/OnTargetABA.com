Sound familiar?

  • Your child pulls you by the hand to the fridge, and you still have to guess what they want.

  • Tantrums and meltdowns go on and on, often over things you never saw coming.

  • They hit, bite or bang their head when upset, and nothing seems to help.

  • You can't look away for a second, because they bolt.

  • You find it hard to trust anyone else to keep them safe.

  • Stores, parks and family visits feel impossible, so you mostly stay home.

  • Potty training feels out of reach, and daily routines leave you exhausted.

The hardest moments often happen when your child has no easy way to tell you what they need.

So we start with communication. At our centers, at home or at school, we help children:

  • Tell you what they want, with words, signs, pictures or their talking device
  • Have fewer tantrums and meltdowns, because they don't have to fight so hard to be understood
  • Ask for help or a break, so hitting, biting and head-banging happen less
  • Stay close and wait, the first steps toward easier outings
  • Learn everyday skills, like using the toilet, little by little
  • Have fun learning, with one-on-one support that puts safety first

We have watched many children say their first words, string words together, make eye contact and point to what they want within their first days with us.
